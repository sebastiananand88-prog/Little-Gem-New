About the Studio

A small print studio
with a lot of type.

I’m Jenny. Little Gem Letterpress is my one-woman studio in Oxfordshire, in an old courtyard of farm buildings on the edge of the Cotswolds — full of vintage type, hand-operated presses, and prints covering every wall.

How it started

One tabletop press and a love of printing.

I started Little Gem Letterpress in 2014 with one tabletop press, half a dozen trays of type, and a love of printing. Since then I’ve accumulated over a hundred different fonts and decorations, learned how to make and use plates from original artwork, mix my own inks, and print on all sorts of papers and materials.

I taught myself the craft through study and a lot of practice, and over the years I’ve joined forces with designers, artists and illustrators to expand what I can print. But it’s still just me at the press — I set every piece of type, mix every colour, feed every sheet through by hand, and check every print before it leaves the studio. When you buy something from me, you’re buying from the person who made it, one impression at a time.

Because nothing here is automated, no two prints are ever quite identical. There’ll be tiny variations across a run — and that isn’t a flaw, it’s the charm of the thing. The greatest compliment I get is people coming back: a reprint, news of how their business is doing, or something new to make together. I never tire of it.

What letterpress is

Letterpress is a traditional printing method: metal or wooden type, onto which paper is pressed, leaving an ink impression on the page. By adjusting the pressure, that impression can be varied from a light ‘kiss’ to a deep imprint in the texture of the paper.

Over the past decade there’s been a real revival in the craft — the gratifyingly tactile effect of a deep, crisp impression, the varying textures left by wood, metal and even plastic type, and the artistic possibilities of overlapping layers of ink, built up one colour at a time. It’s the opposite of the flat sameness of digital printing: a pleasing physical realness, and a small escape from the online world.
